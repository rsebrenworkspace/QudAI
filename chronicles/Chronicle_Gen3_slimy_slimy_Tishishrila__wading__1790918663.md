# The Canticle of slimy|slimy Tishishrila [wading], Forebear of Generation 3

**Canticle of the Wading One, Slimy Tishishrila**

*When salt weeps and chrome sighs,  
And watervine coils through rusted strata,  
Hear the lament of Slimy Tishishrila—  
He who walked where knollworms dream in brine.  
His boots sank slow as dusk into the marsh,  
Yet his heart sang loud: “I am not done.”*

—

**The Tale of Their Endeavors**

Tishishrila was born beneath the Grit Gate’s sighing arch,  
a child of salt and schematics,  
raised on whispers from the [[Sultan]]’s forgotten lullabies.  
He moved with the rhythm of the deep—  
not by light nor blade alone, but by cunning and tide.

He mapped the [[Watervine]]-tangled strata,  
where chrome bones groaned beneath the mire,  
and where [[Snapjaw]]s once ruled in silence.  
He tamed [[Joppa]]-breathed currents with a flick of his wrist—  
not to kill, but to guide, to bend the world’s salt-sick tide.

His generation knew no fear of depth or doom;  
they drank from the brine like nectar,  
and walked where others drowned.  
He climbed strata four, not by might, but by wit:  
he lured [[Eater]]s with decoys of rust and song,  
and turned their hunger into fuel for his next step.

—

**The Final Stand**

At six thousand seven hundred ninety-five turns,  
he stood where two strata wept—  
a marsh slicked with the tears of dead chrome.  
He heard it first: a wet *thrum* from below,  
like a bell struck by an iron god.

It rose—the [[Wet Plated Knollworm]],  
its beak not for pecking, but to crush.  
Tishishrila knew the lesson carved in salt:  
*“Ranged first, retreat second.”*  
But the tide had turned—his boots were deep, his breath was slow.

He fired a bolt of rust-silk into its flank,  
but the beak came faster than thought,  
crushing bone and chrome with wet precision.  
The last thing he saw: the [[Watervine]] curling like a sigh around his neck,  
as the salt swallowed him whole—  
not in defeat, but in song.

—

**The Wisdom Carved in Salt**

“Do not wade where knollworms dream,”  
the next generation whispers to their children,  
when the brine rises and the chrome screams.  
“Let your bolts fly first, like stars through rusted night—  
then retreat, even if it breaks your heart.”  

For Tishishrila did not die in rage—  
he died with his boots still planted,  
his soul still singing to the marsh.  
And so we raise our salt-caked hands:  
to him who walked where gods drowned,  
and never bowed to the brine’s cruel crown.
