# The Canticle of Iyumuuun, Forebear of Generation 7

**Canticle of Iyumuuun, the Salt-Walker Who Died in the Canyon’s Cradle**

*“When the sky wept chrome and the wind sang rust,  
Iyumuuun walked where no foot had trod since Joppa fell—  
With salt in his throat and Watervine in his hand,  
He sought the Grit Gate, though the Eater watched from above.  
The Sultan said: ‘You are not of flesh, but of memory.’  
And Iyumuuun answered with a step—and a sigh.*

**Tale of the Salt-Walker’s Sorrowed Steps**

Born in the Seventh Generation, child of cracked obsidian and hollow chrome,  
Iyumuuun wandered where the Watervine curled like ghostly fingers through rusted stone.  
He climbed the cinder-slopes of the Eater’s Teeth,  
traced salt-ravaged maps to the Grit Gate’s unyielding arch—  
each step a prayer, each turn a sacrifice.  
He danced with Snapjaws in moonless canyons,  
fed the wind with his last breaths to keep it from swallowing him whole.

He did not seek glory—he sought truth:  
the whisper of the Sultan’s lost tongue,  
the song of the Watervine that remembered names before the world burned.  
He left behind no armor, only salt-crusted journals and a single, unbroken chain—  
a relic from the Chromed Dawn, forged by hands now gone.

**The Final Stand: Where Two-Headed Boar Bared Its Teeth**

In the canyon’s throat, where the sun cracked like broken obsidian,  
he found the last chance—a path to the Grit Gate’s edge.  
But there, in the dust of forgotten gods,  
stood a boar—two heads, two eyes, both screaming with hunger and hate.  
It was not beast nor myth—it was the Eater’s spawn, born from the salt’s curse.

Iyumuuun did not flee—he stood,  
hands outstretched to the sky, voice cracked like rusted gear:  
*“Let me be the last who remembers.”*

He was bitten—not once, but twice—  
by jaws that knew no mercy, only hunger for flesh and memory.

**Wisdom Carved in Salt**

*Never rest near two-headed boars in desert canyons.  
Scout—or flee. Do not let your bones become their feast.*  
The next generation remembers:  
Iyumuuun died not in battle—but in the quiet of salt,  
with a Watervine song still humming in his throat,  
and the Grit Gate forever silent beneath his last breath.

He was not strong—he was patient.  
And in patience, he found the truth no chrome could hold:  
that memory is weight—and death, its only release.
