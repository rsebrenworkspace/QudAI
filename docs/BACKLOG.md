# QudAI: Backlog (ideas that are not scheduled work yet)

> The project is expected to grow. This file is where growth is **captured without derailing the current stage**.
> An idea here is not a task. Agents do not start backlog items unless a human has promoted them (see section 1).
> Status per idea is tracked in the `Backlog` sheet of `QudAI_Roadmap.xlsx`. This file holds the details agents need.
> Confidence tags: `[verified in code @c50b3c2]` (read in the code), `[from docs]` (a doc says so, not checked), `[unknown]` (needs research).

## 1. How an idea becomes work

| Step | Status | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Idea** | Captured here in your words, with what exists today and what is unknown. |
| 2 | **Researched** | A research card (`docs/tasks/RESEARCH_TEMPLATE.md`) has answered the engine questions. Findings are in `ENGINE_INTERNALS.md`, tagged. |
| 3 | **Defined** | Measurable exit criteria are written, dependencies are named, and a size estimate exists from measured usage (a small card cost about 4% of the hourly meter on 2026-10-04). |
| 4 | **Scheduled** | A numbered stage gets it, usually because the previous stage's exit criteria are met or close. |

Why: new features are where new loop bugs come from (equip/unequip loops, accidental dialogue confirms, chat spam). A stable base first, then a researched feature, keeps the cost of each step small.

### B11. A standard for who gets proselytized (promoted by the human 2026-10-08 for stage 1 only: the outcome log; built as T-1.54 on `feat/proselytize-log`)
- **Your words:** "If it can be humanoid using that ability on Strong foes could assist with future conflicts. Should we create a standard for the targets that should be targeted. Obviously higher level mobs increase the risk due to resistance to the ability."
- **Exists today:** ENGINE_INTERNALS 14.20: the attack is Ego plus level against the target's mental armor plus level, and the level gap above you counts one for one; hostile and neutral targets are both valid; cooldown 25. The brain recruits by two rules, a calm approach (`MOVE` toward a target 2 tiles away when healthy, one enemy at most, not Tough or worse) and the LLM or the Esper fallback adjacent in a fight; there is no outcome log, and 26 attempts so far have no recorded result. Joppa's named NPCs are protected by a hand-written list, but shopkeepers such as Nima Ruda and Kuyukas are not (HANDOFF issue 79).
- **Proposed standard (not built):** (1) hard gates: the engine's `can_proselytize`, not a companion, never a settlement resident or conversational peaceful NPC, never a rooted creature; (2) power gate by the level gap: freely when the target is at or below your level, when calm up to your Ego modifier above it, never beyond that until measured odds say otherwise; (3) value: prefer the highest level that passes the gate (more health and damage) and creatures with ranged or special attacks, which humanoids often have; (4) safety: HP at least 70 percent, no other hostile within 5, one attempt per cooldown, a small companion cap; (5) log every attempt to `memory/proselytize_log.jsonl` (target, its level, our level, Ego modifier, hostile or not, outcome read from the companion list) and after about 20 records replace the guessed gap limits with measured ones.
- **Needs from the game side:** export the target's `MA` per entity (engine truth, `Stats.GetCombatMA`); `CanBeProselytized` to refuse peaceful conversational NPCs (issue 79).
- **Depends on:** the next C# batch; the turret fix settling.
- **Size (my guess):** the log and the gap rule are small; the MA export small (C# restart); tuning needs about 20 real attempts.
- **Risks:** a failed attempt on a hostile target costs a melee round; a recruited strong creature that keeps dying; companions in the way of our own beams (rule R8 already protects them); recruiting something that matters to a quest (none found).

### B14. Put our abilities into the threat score (idea from the human, 2026-10-08; not promoted yet, "we should revisit it")

### B15. Artifacts: items that are used to create game-changing items and abilities (idea from the human, 2026-10-08; not promoted yet)
- **Your words:** "Artifacts ... can be interacted with to create game changing items and abilities."
- **Exists today:** the item catalog has an `artifact` group but it holds only 2 entries (high-powered magnet, magnetic bottle), so the word almost certainly covers more than that group `[verified in data/items.json]`. Nothing in the brain deals with artifacts on purpose; unidentified items are never equipped or used by the inventory code, and quest items, relics and reputation trophies are protected from dropping (catalog `protect`).
- **First example and what the game says `[verified in game data 2026-10-08: Manual.xml topic "Artifacts", ObjectBlueprints/Data.xml]`:** the human found a "weird artifact" (2 lbs) dropped by a killed Waydroid, and an "odd trinket" earlier. Those are the placeholder names of **unidentified** items (`BaseUnknown`: "weird artifact"; `UnknownOddTrinket`: "odd trinket"; each has `Examiner` removed). The manual: artifacts are items so technologically complex that few understand them; **you examine one to try to identify it**, success is based on your Intelligence, success narrows what it could be and eventually reveals it, **each failure has a small chance of breaking it**. Tonics count as artifacts and need identifying too; the Tinker skill can concoct tonics and works with "bits". So "interact" most likely means examine/identify first, then use or disassemble or build with Tinkering `[unverified: which interaction creates items and abilities]`.
- **Unknown `[unknown]`:** what else counts as an artifact (relics, chrome, ancient technology); which can be "interacted with" and what the interaction is (activate, apply, examine, disassemble, combine at a machine or with a skill such as Tinkering); what they produce (items, mutations or abilities, cybernetics); what it costs; where they are found; whether an interaction can be driven headlessly or needs a menu (see B1: the recipe menu was avoided on purpose).
- **Plan, smallest first (not started):** a research card in the style of `docs/tasks/R-1-cooking-recipes-research.md`: list the artifact-like items and their `Description`/`Apply` parts in the blueprints, find the engine commands behind the interactions, and write what each one costs and gives. Only then decide whether the brain should do anything with them (the first step would be to stop dropping or ignoring the valuable ones).
- **Depends on:** the inventory code staying stable (several equip/drop loops lived there), the item catalog, B1 (headless menus) and B8 (trade value) for any item that is better sold.
- **Size (my guess):** research medium; implementation unknown until the interactions are known.
- **Risks:** the interactions may need menus (the same trap as cooking); an artifact used blindly can be harmful or destroy something valuable; theft or owned items are off limits (see B6 and the ethics note).

### B16. Pick up and use the equipment that kills leave on the ground (observation from the human, 2026-10-09; promoted by the human the same day and built on `feat/ground-equipment`, C# changed, awaiting a game test)

- **Evidence `[verified in state and trace, Gen 27, 2026-10-09]`:** the run lasted 3,459 turns, reached level 5 (32 HP) and ended with the same kit as at level 1: `av` 1, `dv` 0, a staff doing 1d2 (penetration -1). Gear, not tactics, is the largest lever this character has not used.

Chests are handled separately (verified in game 2026-10-09). The equip and drop logic (`item_scoring`, `choose_inventory_action`, `EQUIP_ITEM`, `DROP_ITEMS`) already works on what is CARRIED.

- **Plan, smallest first:** (1) C# exports `ground_items`: unowned, takeable, non-corpse items within about 8 cells (id, blueprint, name, distance, cell, weight), never in a settlement, never one the mod itself dropped (mark it on `DROP_ITEMS`); (2) a C# `TAKE_ITEM:<id>` command for an item on the player's or an adjacent cell (the engine's `TakeObject`, as the loot step already does for chests); (3) Python scores each ground item with the SAME scorer as the inventory (`item_scoring.score_item`, `choose_equips`) and walks to and takes one only when it would be equipped as a clear upgrade (so it never takes what the drop rule would drop again); the existing `EQUIP_ITEM` step then equips it; (4) wish test: wish `Chain Mail` or a long sword near him with the AI engaged and see whether he picks it up and wears it.

- **Depends on:** the item scorer and the inventory export staying stable; a clean compile of the C# change (needs a Qud restart); B6's ethics rule (never take owned items).
- **Size (my guess):** C# small to medium, Python small, tests medium.
- **Risks:** a pickup and drop loop if the take rule and the drop rule disagree (so they must share one scorer, and a taken item must never be a drop candidate in the same state); carrying too much weight; walking toward loot while hostiles are near (Phase A only, with the same guards as the loot step); equipping while in danger.
